4.1 Repeated EigenValues.

Note

Theorem 4.1 (Theorem) Suppose \(\lambda\) is a repeated eigenvalue for the system:

\[\frac{dx}{dt}=ax+by\] \[\frac{dy}{dt}=cx+dy\]

with eigenvector \(K\), then

\[\vec{x_1} = Ke^{\lambda t}\] \[\vec{x_2} = K\,t\,e^{\lambda t}+Pe^{\lambda t}\] Where \(P\) is to be determined from

\[\begin{bmatrix}a-\lambda & b\\ c& d-\lambda\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}p_1\\p_2\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}k_1\\k_2\end{bmatrix}\] is a fundamental set.

4.1.1 Example 1:

TipExample

Find the general solution to

\[\frac{dx}{dt}=4x-y\] \[\frac{dy}{dt}=x+2y\]

CautionSolution

4.1.2 eigenvalues:

\[\begin{bmatrix}4-\lambda & -1\\ 1& 2-\lambda\end{bmatrix}=0\]

\[(4-\lambda)(2-\lambda)+1=0\]

\[8-6\lambda+\lambda^2+1=0\] \[\lambda^2-6\lambda+9=0\] \[(\lambda-3)^2=0\]

4.1.3 eigenvectors:

\(\boxed{\lambda =3}:\)

\[\begin{bmatrix}4-3 & -1\\ 1& 2-3\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}k_1\\k_2\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}0\\0\end{bmatrix}\]

\[k_1-k_2=0\] \[k_1=k_2\] \[K=\begin{bmatrix}1\\1\end{bmatrix}\]

4.1.4 Fundamental set:

\(\vec{x_1}\) is done:

\[\vec{x_1}=Ke^{\lambda t}= \begin{bmatrix}1\\1\end{bmatrix}e^{3t} \] for \(\vec{x_2}\) we need to solve:

\[\begin{bmatrix}1 & -1\\ 1& -1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}p_1\\p_2\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}k_1\\k_2\end{bmatrix}\] \[\begin{bmatrix}1 & -1\\ 1& -1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}p_1\\p_2\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}1\\1\end{bmatrix}\] \[p_1-p_2=1\] \[p_1=1+p_2\] \[P=\begin{bmatrix}2\\1\end{bmatrix}\]

Therefore:

\[\vec{x_2}=e^{3t}\,t\,\begin{bmatrix}1\\1\end{bmatrix}+e^{3t}\begin{bmatrix}2\\1\end{bmatrix}\]

4.1.5 General Solution:

\[\begin{bmatrix}x\\y\end{bmatrix}=c_1e^{3t}\begin{bmatrix}1\\1\end{bmatrix}+c_2\left(t\,e^{3t}\begin{bmatrix}1\\1\end{bmatrix}+e^{3t}\begin{bmatrix}2\\1\end{bmatrix}\right)\]

4.1.6 Example 2:

TipExample

\[\frac{dx}{dt}=y\] \[\frac{dy}{dt}=-9x+6y\]

CautionSolution

\[\begin{vmatrix}0-\lambda & 1\\ -9&6-\lambda\end{vmatrix}\]

\[-6\lambda+\lambda^2+9=0\] \[(\lambda-3)^2=0\]

\[\begin{bmatrix}-3 & 1\\ -9& 3\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}k_1\\k_2\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}0\\0\end{bmatrix}\]

\[-3k_1+k_2=0\] \[K=\begin{bmatrix}1\\3\end{bmatrix}\]

\[\vec{x_1}=Ke^{\lambda t}= e^{3t}\begin{bmatrix}1\\3\end{bmatrix}\]

\[\begin{bmatrix}-3 & 1\\ -9& 3\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}p_1\\p_2\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}1\\3\end{bmatrix}\] \[-3p_1+p_2=1\] \[p_2=1+3p_1\] \[P=\begin{bmatrix}1\\4\end{bmatrix}\]

\[\vec{x_2}=t\,e^{3t}\begin{bmatrix}1\\3\end{bmatrix}+e^{3t}\begin{bmatrix}1\\4\end{bmatrix}\]

Then the general solution is:

\[\begin{bmatrix}x\\y\end{bmatrix}=c_1e^{3t}\begin{bmatrix}1\\3\end{bmatrix}+c_2\left(t\,e^{3t}\begin{bmatrix}1\\3\end{bmatrix}+e^{3t}\begin{bmatrix}1\\4\end{bmatrix}\right)\]
